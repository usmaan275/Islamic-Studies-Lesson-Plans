# Lesson Aim

Today is the beginning of a new Year 9 Islamic Studies course.

The main question of the lesson is:

> **Why am I a Muslim?**

Students should begin to understand that being Muslim cannot simply mean:

- “Because my parents are Muslim.”
- “Because I was born Muslim.”
- “Because my family is Muslim.”
- “Because everyone around me is Muslim.”

Those may explain **how** someone became Muslim, but they do not fully answer **why Islam is true**.

Over the coming lessons, we will explore three major reasons:

1. **Allāh** — His existence, power, creation and control
2. **The Qurʾān** — its preservation, message and miraculous nature
3. **The Prophet Muḥammad ﷺ** — his character, life, miracles and prophecies

Today's lesson focuses mainly on **Allāh**.

## Surah al-Nūr 24:39 — The Mirage

Display the āyah:

> **وَٱلَّذِینَ كَفَرُوۤا۟ أَعۡمَـٰلُهُمۡ كَسَرَابِۭ بِقِیعَةࣲ یَحۡسَبُهُ ٱللَّمۡـَٔانُ مَاۤءً حَتَّىٰۤ إِذَا جَاۤءَهُۥ لَمۡ یَجِدۡهُ شَیۡـࣰٔا وَوَجَدَ ٱللَّهَ عِندَهُۥ فَوَفَّىٰهُ حِسَابَهُۥۗ وَٱللَّهُ سَرِیعُ ٱلۡحِسَابِ﴿ ٣٩ ﴾**

> “As for the disbelievers, their deeds are like a mirage in a desert, which the thirsty perceive as water, but when they approach it, they find it to be nothing. Instead, they find Allah there ˹in the Hereafter, ready˺ to settle their account. And Allah is swift in reckoning.”
>
> — An-Nūr 24:39

Give students a few minutes to read the āyah and identify:

- What is a **mirage**?
- Why does a thirsty person think it is water?
- Why does Allah compare it to a **desert**?
- What happens when the person finally reaches it?
- What do you think the āyah is teaching us about the things people chase in this life?

Allow students to discuss in pairs/small groups before taking answers.

# 5:05–5:15 | Breaking Down the Āyah

## 1. كَسَرَابٍ بِقِيعَةٍ
### “Like a mirage in a desert”

Explain:

A **mirage** looks like something valuable — usually water — but it isn't actually there.

It can be:

- attractive
- exciting
- convincing
- something you desperately want

But when you finally reach it:

> **There is nothing there.**

The Qurʾān places this mirage in a **desert**.

A desert is barren.

There is very little:

- water
- food
- vegetation
- life

So the person desperately wants the mirage to be real.

### Teacher point

This can be compared to **empty ambitions**.

Someone might spend their whole life chasing:

- money
- popularity
- attention
- relationships
- substances
- pleasure
- status
- social media approval

They may think:

> “Once I get that, I'll finally be happy.”

But then they reach it.

And there is still something missing.

---

# 2. يَحْسَبُهُ الظَّمْآنُ مَاءً
### “The thirsty person thinks it is water”

Ask the students:

> Why does the thirsty person think the mirage is real?

It isn't necessarily because:

- there is something wrong with his eyes
- there is not enough light
- he is stupid

He is **thirsty**.

His condition is affecting how he sees what is in front of him.

His inner need is influencing his perception.

### Make the connection

This is similar to someone overwhelmed by their desires.

The person may know:

> “I shouldn't vape.”

Yet they vape.

They may know:

> “I shouldn't smoke.”

Yet they smoke.

They may know:

> “I shouldn't speak to people in this way.”

Yet they continue.

They may know:

> “This isn't good for me.”

Yet they chase it anyway.

The problem isn't necessarily that they don't know the truth.

The problem is that their **desires have become stronger than their self-control**.

---

# Important Teacher Point

Do NOT simply tell students:

> “People who do bad things are stupid.”

Instead explain:

> A person can know something is wrong and still allow their desires to overpower their knowledge.

The Qurʾān is teaching us to examine what is happening **inside ourselves**.

---

# 3. حَتَّىٰ إِذَا جَاءَهُ لَمْ يَجِدْهُ شَيْئًا
### “When he reaches it, he finds nothing”

Ask:

> What happens when the thirsty person finally reaches the mirage?

Nothing.

No water.

No satisfaction.

No reward for the journey.

Everything he thought he was going to find is gone.

### Connect this to the Dunyā

Many things in this world feel amazing for a moment.

But ask:

> How long does the feeling actually last?

The excitement of:

- buying something
- getting likes
- winning
- becoming popular
- getting attention
- trying something forbidden

can disappear very quickly.

The person then needs the **next thing**.

And then the next.

And the next.

The mirage keeps moving.

---

# 4. وَوَجَدَ اللَّهَ عِندَهُ
### “And he finds Allah there”

This is the frightening part of the āyah.

He thought he was travelling towards water.

Instead, he finds Allah.

The person has reached the end of the journey.

Everything he chased is gone.

And now he stands before Allah.

### Teacher message

Tell the students:

> Allah is giving us the opportunity to recognise the mirage **before we reach it**.

You can turn back.

You can change direction.

You can stop chasing things that will leave you empty.

You can return to Allah **now**.

---

# 5. فَوَفَّاهُ حِسَابَهُ
### “So He will settle his account”

The ending reminds us:

Everything will eventually be accounted for.

Every:

- choice
- action
- intention
- sacrifice
- sin
- good deed

will matter.

And Allah is:

> **وَاللَّهُ سَرِيعُ الْحِسَابِ**
>
> “Swift in reckoning.”

---

# Reflection

Then explain:

The Qurʾān isn't just talking about someone else.

It is asking **us**:

> What are you chasing?

# 5:43–5:45 | 1. THE PAST — CREATION

## “Look at what Allah has already done.”

Ask students:

> Look around you.
>
> Where did all of this come from?

---

# The Mobile Phone Analogy

Hold up a phone.

Ask:

> If I told you this phone suddenly appeared out of absolutely nothing, would you believe me?

Obviously not.

Then say:

> What if I smashed it into hundreds of pieces and scattered those pieces across the room?
>
> And then I told you:
>
> “Just wait long enough and eventually all these pieces will randomly come together in exactly the right arrangement, reconnect, turn on, and become a fully functioning phone.”

Would they believe you?

Why not?

Because:

- the phone is complex
- the parts have specific functions
- the parts must be arranged correctly
- the system has to work together

---

# Apply the Analogy to the Universe

Then say:

> If we wouldn't believe that a phone could organise itself by accident, how can we look at something infinitely more complex — the entire universe — and simply assume that it organised itself without a Creator?

The universe isn't just a pile of matter.

It contains:

- galaxies
- stars
- planets
- atoms
- physical laws
- gravity
- light
- chemistry
- life

And everything operates according to consistent rules.

---

# The Human Body

Now bring the discussion closer:

> You don't even have to look at the universe.
>
> Look at yourself.

Give examples:

### The brain

The human brain contains roughly **86 billion neurons**.

Those neurons communicate through incredibly complex networks.

Ask:

> Did you design your own brain?

No.

> Did your parents design it?

No.

Yet you have a system capable of:

- thinking
- remembering
- speaking
- imagining
- calculating
- feeling emotions

---

# Another Amazing Example — Blood Vessels

Explain:

The human body has roughly **60,000 miles of blood vessels**.

That is enough to go around the Earth more than twice.

And all of this exists inside one human body.

Ask:

> Who designed this system?

---

# The Cosmos — The Sun & Moon

Give the students another example:

The Sun is roughly **400 times wider than the Moon**.

But the Sun is also roughly **400 times farther away from Earth than the Moon**.

Because of this remarkable coincidence, they appear approximately the same size in our sky.

This is why total solar eclipses can occur.

Tell students:

> We don't worship the Sun.
>
> We don't worship the Moon.
>
> We worship the One who created both.

---

# Earth's Position

Earth exists within a very particular range of conditions that allow life as we know it.

If Earth were substantially closer to the Sun:

- temperatures would increase dramatically
- liquid water would become much harder to maintain

If substantially farther away:

- temperatures would fall dramatically
- liquid water would become much harder to maintain

We also have:

- an atmosphere
- suitable pressure
- abundant liquid water
- protection from harmful radiation
- conditions suitable for life

### Teacher point

Don't tell students:

> “One tiny change and humans would definitely instantly die.”

Instead say:

> The universe contains an extraordinary number of finely balanced conditions that allow life to exist.

---

# 5:45 | Key Argument — Where Does Everything Come From?

Ask:

> If everything that begins to exist needs an explanation, what explains the universe?

The universe cannot simply be its own creator.

Why?

Because something cannot create itself **before it exists**.

---

# The Infinite Regression Example

Explain simply:

Imagine I say:

> “I'll give you £100. Just wait.”

You ask:

> “When?”

I say:

> “After infinity.”

Would you ever receive it?

No.

You cannot wait through an actual infinity to reach a starting point.

Therefore, if everything required something before it, and that something required something before it forever, we would never arrive at the present.

So there must ultimately be a **necessary, uncaused reality** upon which everything else depends.

In Islamic belief:

> **That is Allah.**

Allah did not need someone to create Him.

He is:

> **Al-Awwal — The First**

> **Al-Ghani — The One completely independent of creation**

---

# 5:55–6:00 | The Fitrah — Something Inside Us

Now move from the outside world to the inside.

Ask:

> Why do human beings naturally recognise certain things as good and certain things as evil?

Give a simple example.

Imagine a superhero film.

Nobody has to stop the film and say:

> “Class, remember: you must support the good guy and dislike the evil villain.”

Most people naturally support:

- justice
- bravery
- protecting the innocent
- truth

And dislike:

- betrayal
- oppression
- cruelty

---

# Fitrah

Explain:

Allah created human beings upon a natural disposition — **fiṭrah**.

The fiṭrah inclines us towards:

- recognising our Creator
- truth
- goodness
- morality

But the fiṭrah can become covered or corrupted.

Things such as:

- desires
- peer pressure
- bad environments
- repeated sins
- propaganda
- immoral social norms

can distort how someone sees right and wrong.

### Connect it back to the mirage

This is exactly why the opening āyah is so powerful.

The problem isn't always:

> “I don't have enough information.”

Sometimes the problem is:

> **“I don't want to accept what the truth requires from me.”**

---

# 6:00–6:05 | 2. THE PRESENT — Allah Is Sustaining You Now

Tell students:

> We have looked at what Allah has done in the past.
>
> Now look at what Allah is doing **right now**.

Ask everyone:

> Is your heart beating?

Yes.

Then:

> Did you consciously make your heart beat?

No.

> Can you decide to stop your heart for ten seconds?

No.

---

# Your Heart

Say:

> Every second your heart continues beating without you controlling it.

Allah is sustaining you at every moment.

Your life continues because Allah allows it to continue.

---

# The Soul

Explain:

The body can remain physically present after death:

- the eyes remain, but they no longer see
- the ears remain, but they no longer hear
- the heart may still physically exist, but it no longer sustains life
- the limbs remain, but they no longer move

What has changed?

The soul has left.

The command of Allah has been taken away.

---

# Food

Ask:

> Does food guarantee nourishment?

No.

Sometimes someone eats a huge meal and is still hungry.

Sometimes someone eats very little and feels satisfied.

Food is a means.

But Allah is the One who actually gives nourishment.

---

# Sleep

Ask:

> Does sleep guarantee that you will wake up feeling energetic?

No.

Sometimes:

- you sleep for eight hours and still feel exhausted
- you sleep for four hours and somehow feel wide awake

Sleep is a means.

But Allah is the One who gives energy.

---

# The Sun

Ask:

> If the sun is shining, does that guarantee that you will feel warm?

No.

We can stand outside on a sunny day and still feel cold.

The sun is a means.

But Allah is the One who creates the effect.

---

# Key Sentence

Put this on the board:

> **Food does not guarantee nourishment.**
>
> **Sleep does not guarantee rest.**
>
> **The sun does not guarantee warmth.**
>
> **These effects only occur by the command of Allah.**
>
> **Allah is sustaining us at every moment.**

---

# 6:05–6:10 | Lā Ilāha Illā Allāh — The Conclusion

Now bring everything together.

Write:

> **لَا إِلَٰهَ إِلَّا اللَّهُ**
>
> **Lā ilāha illā Allāh**

Explain:

It means:

> **There is no deity worthy of worship except Allah.**

But notice how the kalimah begins:

> **لَا**

“No.”

First we **negate**.

We reject the illusion.

We reject the idea that:

- money is ultimately what sustains us
- food independently sustains us
- sleep independently gives us energy
- the sun independently gives us warmth
- people control our destiny
- creation has power independent of Allah

Then we affirm:

> **إِلَّا اللَّهُ**
>
> **Except Allah.**

Allah is the One behind everything.

---

Return to the opening āyah:

> The thirsty person sees the mirage and thinks:
>
> **“That's where my water is.”**

But when he reaches it:

> **There is nothing there.**

Tell students:

> Don't spend your life chasing mirages.

Don't allow:

- desires
- friends
- trends
- popularity
- money
- temporary pleasure

to decide what you believe is valuable.

The evidence is all around you.

The problem isn't necessarily a lack of light.

The problem isn't necessarily your eyes.

Sometimes the problem is what is happening **inside you**.

You already know many things are right.

You already know many things are wrong.

The challenge is developing the self-control to follow what you know is true.

# Teacher's Core Takeaways

By the end of today's lesson, students should leave understanding:

- Creation points us towards a Creator.
- The universe cannot be its own ultimate explanation.
- Allah is independent and uncreated.
- Human beings possess a natural **fiṭrah**.
- Our desires can distort how we perceive truth.
- Allah is not only the Creator — He is constantly sustaining creation.
- Food, sleep and the sun are means, but Allah creates their effects.
- Everything ultimately depends upon Allah.
- We therefore worship Allah alone.
- Being Muslim should be something we understand and believe for ourselves, not merely something inherited from our families.
- The “mirage” of the dunya can look attractive while ultimately leaving a person empty.
- The goal is to recognise the truth **before** standing before Allah.

# PART 1 — THE STARTER

## 5:00–5:15 — One Extra Word: Does It Change Anything?

### SLIDE 1 — Compare the two phrases

**Slide description for Gemini:**

Create a clean, minimalistic 16:9 slide split vertically into two equal halves. Use a subtle cream background with dark green and charcoal text. The left side should have the first Arabic phrase, large and beautifully typeset; the right side should have the second phrase. Place the English translations underneath each phrase. Use a thin central divider. At the bottom, put three short discussion questions in a horizontal row. Avoid clutter, decorative borders or excessive Islamic motifs.

**Left side:**

### مَا فِي السَّمَاوَاتِ وَالْأَرْضِ

*Everything in the heavens and the earth.*

**Right side:**

### مَا فِي السَّمَاوَاتِ وَمَا فِي الْأَرْضِ

*Everything in the heavens and everything on the earth.*

**Questions at the bottom:**

1. What is the difference?
2. Why might Allah use the extra **ما**?
3. How might this change relate to what comes next in the Qur'an?

### Teacher instructions

Give students approximately **3–4 minutes** to discuss in their tables.

Say:

> "Look carefully. The phrases are almost identical."

> "One of them repeats a word that the other doesn't."

> "Why would Allah use one version in one place and another version somewhere else?"

> "Don't just tell me what the difference is. Tell me why the difference might matter."

Ask each table for its ideas. Encourage them to explain their reasoning rather than guessing the answer you want.

### Teacher explanation

The first phrase groups the expression together:

**"Whatever is in the heavens and the earth."**

The second explicitly repeats the word **ما**:

**"Whatever is in the heavens, and whatever is on the earth."**

That repetition can give the wording a more divided, detailed or emphatic feel. It draws attention separately to the two parts of creation.

Today we are going to investigate a pattern in the Qur'an: in some passages, the repeated wording appears before a discussion of earthly events, while the more concise wording appears in a passage focusing on Allah's attributes and greatness.

**Important teacher note:** This is a pattern to investigate in context, not a universal grammatical rule. The Qur'an uses both expressions in different contexts, so students should notice the relationship rather than assume the wording determines the subject every time.

## SLIDE 2 — Example 1: Surah al-Hadid

**Slide description for Gemini:**

Create a split layout. Put the Arabic verses in a large, elegant Arabic text block on the left, with the English translation in a separate block at the bottom of the slide. On the right, use three short labels: **Creation**, **Allah's kingdom**, **Allah's power**. Keep the focus on the concise phrase in verse 1. Do not put English between the Arabic lines.

### Arabic

سَبَّحَ لِلَّهِ مَا فِي السَّمَاوَاتِ وَالْأَرْضِ ۖ وَهُوَ الْعَزِيزُ الْحَكِيمُ ۝١

لَهُ مُلْكُ السَّمَاوَاتِ وَالْأَرْضِ ۖ يُحْيِي وَيُمِيتُ ۖ وَهُوَ عَلَىٰ كُلِّ شَيْءٍ قَدِيرٌ ۝٢

### English translation

**Al-Hadid 57:1–2**

"Whatever is in the heavens and the earth glorifies Allah, for He is the Almighty, All-Wise."

"To Him belongs the kingdom of the heavens and the earth. He gives life and causes death. And He is Most Capable of everything."

### Teacher notes

Here, the wording is concise:

**مَا فِي السَّمَاوَاتِ وَالْأَرْضِ**

Allah then speaks about His own greatness:

* He is the Almighty and All-Wise.
* The kingdom belongs to Him.
* He gives life and causes death.
* He has power over everything.

Ask:

> "What is the main focus of these verses?"

Expected answer: **Allah's greatness, ownership and power.**

The wording gathers the heavens and earth into one expression as the passage declares Allah's complete dominion.

## SLIDE 3 — Example 2: Surah al-Hashr

**Slide description for Gemini:**

Use the same visual template as Slide 2 to make the comparison easy. Highlight the repeated **وَمَا فِي الْأَرْضِ** in verse 1. On the right, show a subtle silhouette of old Madinah buildings or a simple map-style illustration of a walled settlement. Keep the imagery historical and restrained, not dramatic or violent.

### Arabic

سَبَّحَ لِلَّهِ مَا فِي السَّمَاوَاتِ وَمَا فِي الْأَرْضِ ۖ وَهُوَ الْعَزِيزُ الْحَكِيمُ ۝١

هُوَ الَّذِي أَخْرَجَ الَّذِينَ كَفَرُوا مِنْ أَهْلِ الْكِتَابِ مِنْ دِيَارِهِمْ لِأَوَّلِ الْحَشْرِ ۚ مَا ظَنَنْتُمْ أَنْ يَخْرُجُوا وَظَنُّوا أَنَّهُمْ مَانِعَتُهُمْ حُصُونُهُمْ مِنَ اللَّهِ فَأَتَاهُمُ اللَّهُ مِنْ حَيْثُ لَمْ يَحْتَسِبُوا ۖ وَقَذَفَ فِي قُلُوبِهِمُ الرُّعْبَ ۚ يُخْرِبُونَ بُيُوتَهُمْ بِأَيْدِيهِمْ وَأَيْدِي الْمُؤْمِنِينَ ۚ فَاعْتَبِرُوا يَا أُولِي الْأَبْصَارِ ۝٢

### English translation

**Al-Hashr 59:1–2**

"Whatever is in the heavens and whatever is on the earth glorifies Allah. For He is the Almighty, All-Wise."

"He is the One Who expelled the disbelievers of the People of the Book from their homes for their first banishment. You never thought they would go. And they thought their strongholds would put them out of Allah's reach. But Allah's decree came upon them from where they never expected. And He cast horror into their hearts so they destroyed their houses with their own hands and the hands of the believers. So take a lesson, O people of insight!"

### Teacher notes

Now we have the repeated wording:

**مَا فِي السَّمَاوَاتِ وَمَا فِي الْأَرْضِ**

And the next verse moves into a specific event on earth: the expulsion of a group from their strongholds in Madinah's surroundings.

Ask:

> "What is the subject of verse 2?"

Expected answer: **An earthly historical event involving people, homes and strongholds.**

Then ask:

> "What lesson does Allah tell us to take from it?"

Expected answer: **Do not assume that your worldly strength or security can protect you from Allah's decree.**

Explain that the repeated wording is consistent with a more explicit distinction between the heavens and the earth, while the following verse moves into a detailed earthly event. We should observe the relationship without claiming that the wording alone causes the subject of the next verse.

## SLIDE 4 — Example 3: Surah al-Jumu'ah

**Slide description for Gemini:**

Create a simple two-part slide. The top shows the Arabic of verses 1–2, with the English translation in a separate block underneath. The lower part has two small headings: **Allah is glorified** and **The Messenger is sent**. Use a subtle image of an open Qur'an or a mosque silhouette. Avoid crowding the text.

### Arabic

يُسَبِّحُ لِلَّهِ مَا فِي السَّمَاوَاتِ وَمَا فِي الْأَرْضِ الْمَلِكِ الْقُدُّوسِ الْعَزِيزِ الْحَكِيمِ ۝١

هُوَ الَّذِي بَعَثَ فِي الْأُمِّيِّينَ رَسُولًا مِنْهُمْ يَتْلُو عَلَيْهِمْ آيَاتِهِ وَيُزَكِّيهِمْ وَيُعَلِّمُهُمُ الْكِتَابَ وَالْحِكْمَةَ وَإِنْ كَانُوا مِنْ قَبْلُ لَفِي ضَلَالٍ مُبِينٍ ۝٢

### English translation

**Al-Jumu'ah 62:1–2**

"Whatever is in the heavens and whatever is on the earth constantly glorifies Allah—the King, the Most Holy, the Almighty, the All-Wise."

"He is the One Who raised for the illiterate people a messenger from among themselves—reciting to them His revelations, purifying them, and teaching them the Book and wisdom, for indeed they had previously been clearly astray."

### Teacher notes

This time, after the glorification of Allah, the passage discusses something that happened on earth:

**Allah sent His Messenger ﷺ to a people who had been astray.**

The Prophet ﷺ would:

* Recite Allah's revelations.
* Purify the people.
* Teach them the Book and wisdom.

Ask:

> "Why do you think Allah connects His greatness with the sending of His Messenger?"

Expected answers:

* The Messenger is sent by Allah.
* The message is a mercy and guidance for humanity.
* Allah's guidance changes people's lives and societies.

### Brief additional observation: Surah al-Hashr 22–24

These verses use the repeated wording in verse 1, but the passage later returns to Allah's names and attributes:

* **Al-Rahman, Al-Rahim** — the Most Compassionate, Most Merciful.
* **Al-Malik, Al-Quddus, Al-Salam** — the King, the Most Holy, the Source of Peace.
* **Al-Khaliq, Al-Bari', Al-Musawwir** — the Creator, the Inventor, the Shaper.

This is a useful reminder that the pattern is contextual rather than absolute. Repetition can serve emphasis and detail, while the wider passage determines the subject.

# PART 2 — WHY DO WE BELIEVE THE PROPHET ﷺ?

# 5:30–5:35 — THE LOGICAL CONCLUSION

## SLIDE 6 — Four possibilities

**Slide description for Gemini:**

Create a bold, uncluttered slide with the statement **"Muhammad ﷺ claimed to be a Messenger of Allah"** at the top. Underneath, show four numbered options in a 2-by-2 grid. Keep the first three neutral in colour and give the fourth a subtle green emphasis. Do not reveal the conclusion immediately; reveal each option as the teacher discusses it.

### The four possibilities

1. He was lying.
2. He was genuinely mistaken or deluded.
3. He was influenced by something evil.
4. He was telling the truth.

### Teacher notes

Say:

> "Last week we introduced these four possibilities. Let's go through them carefully."

### Possibility 1: He was lying

Before prophethood, Muhammad ﷺ was known as:

**الصَّادِقُ الْأَمِينُ — Al-Sadiq al-Amin**

The truthful, the trustworthy.

People trusted him with their belongings and relied on his honesty.

Ask:

> "Would someone known for honesty invent a claim about Allah, then spend 23 years suffering because of it?"

He endured persecution, the boycott, the loss of loved ones, attacks and eventually migration from his home.

### Possibility 2: He was mistaken

His message was coherent and consistent:

* Worship one God.
* Be truthful and just.
* Care for the poor and vulnerable.
* Control your desires.
* Prepare for the Hereafter.

His message transformed individuals, families and society.

### Possibility 3: He was influenced by evil

Ask students what kind of teachings he brought.

His message called people to worship Allah, give charity, avoid oppression, forgive, honour family ties and establish justice.

Discuss why this does not fit the idea of a message designed to lead people towards evil.

### Possibility 4: He was telling the truth

Say:

> "When we study his life, his message, his character and the evidence supporting his claim, we must take the fourth possibility seriously: that he really was telling the truth."

**Teacher note:** Present this as the cumulative Islamic argument, not as if a short classroom exercise has mathematically disproved every alternative. The following evidence is what students should examine.

# 5:35–5:41 — HIS CHARACTER: أَخْلَاق

## SLIDE 7 — His character did not change with his circumstances

**Slide description for Gemini:**

Create a powerful, simple slide with three large words stacked vertically:

**HONESTY**
**PATIENCE**
**MERCY**

Use a restrained background showing a desert horizon or an abstract historical Arabian landscape. Do not depict the Prophet ﷺ. Place the phrase **"When he had little power, he showed patience. When he gained power, he showed mercy."** at the bottom.

### Teacher notes

Before prophethood, the Prophet ﷺ was already known as **Al-Sadiq al-Amin**.

He was trusted with people's property and disputes. His reputation for honesty existed before he began calling people to Islam.

Ask:

> "If someone is known for honesty, what would make us believe that he suddenly invented a claim about the Creator of the universe?"

Then explain that his character was tested under extraordinary pressure.

## Story 1 — His patience in Ta'if

The Prophet ﷺ travelled to Ta'if seeking support for his message. Instead, he was rejected and severely mistreated.

Yet his response was not simply to seek revenge. In the well-known narration in Sahih al-Bukhari, when the angel of the mountains offered to crush the people between the mountains, the Prophet ﷺ expressed hope that Allah would bring from their descendants people who worshipped Him alone.

**Ask:**

> "When someone hurts you, is your first instinct to hope that they will change—or to hope that they suffer?"

The Prophet ﷺ hoped for guidance even after being harmed.

## Story 2 — His forgiveness at the conquest of Makkah

The Prophet ﷺ returned to Makkah with strength after years of persecution.

Many of the people he now had power over had opposed him, harmed him or fought against his followers.

Yet the conquest was not an indiscriminate revenge campaign. He extended safety and forgiveness to many of his former enemies.

The famous wording **"Go, for you are free"** is widely associated with the conquest in sīrah literature; be careful not to imply that every detail of the popular quotation has the same hadith status as a report in Sahih al-Bukhari or Sahih Muslim.

Say:

> "When he had little power, he showed patience."

> "When he gained power, he showed mercy."

> "His character did not change depending on whether he was weak or powerful."

## Story 3 — His humility

Despite being the Messenger of Allah ﷺ, he lived simply and served his family.

Authentic reports describe him helping with household tasks. He was not someone who believed that being a leader meant ordinary work was beneath him.

Ask:

> "What tells you more about someone's character: how they treat someone powerful, or how they treat someone who cannot benefit them?"

Expected answer: how they treat people when they have nothing to gain.

### Key message

**His honesty, mercy and humility were not just words. They were visible throughout his life.**

# 5:41–5:45 — HIS RECOGNITION, RESPECT AND IMPACT

## SLIDE 8 — One life. A world transformed.

**Slide description for Gemini:**

Use a central timeline labelled **"23 years of prophethood"**. Around it, show six short themes with simple icons: faith, family, society, justice, finance and governance. Add a small book-cover-style reference to Michael H. Hart's *The 100* in one corner. Do not imply that the book proves Islam; it illustrates the scale of the Prophet's historical influence.

### Teacher notes

In 23 years, the Prophet ﷺ:

* Called people from idol worship to worshipping One God.
* Built brotherhood between people divided by tribal rivalry.
* Taught laws and principles relating to worship, family, society, finance, justice and governance.
* Changed the course of world history.

Mention Michael H. Hart, a non-Muslim author who ranked Muhammad ﷺ first in his book *The 100: A Ranking of the Most Influential Persons in History*.

Explain:

> "This isn't proof of prophethood by itself. It's an example of how someone outside Islam assessed the Prophet's historical influence."

## The companions' love and the Muslim world's connection

Ask:

> "How do we know the companions respected him?"

They listened to him, learned from him, remembered his words and actions, and transmitted reports about his life.

Then bring it into the present:

* Muslims learn how he prayed.
* They follow his example in eating and drinking.
* Many break their fasts with dates, following his Sunnah.
* They study his sayings and actions.
* His name is mentioned in the shahadah and in the daily prayers.
* Muslims around the world send blessings upon him ﷺ.

Explain that Muslims can feel protective when he is insulted because they love and honour him, even though they never met him personally. That love should be expressed in ways consistent with his teachings, including justice, restraint and good character.

### "The most documented man on the planet"

Use careful wording:

> "The Prophet ﷺ is one of the most extensively documented figures of the ancient world. His followers transmitted an extraordinary amount of detail about his words, actions, worship and character."

We should avoid claiming a universally measurable title such as *the most documented man on the planet*. The point is that Muslims have an exceptionally rich body of material about how he lived.

### Memorable line

> **"He wasn't simply remembered. His life became a model that people continue to imitate more than 1,400 years later."**

# 5:45–5:48 — THE HONOUR ALLAH GIVES HIS MESSENGER ﷺ

## SLIDE 9 — Allah saw him looking towards the heavens

**Slide description for Gemini:**

Use a dark, calm sky fading into a soft golden light. Do not depict the Prophet ﷺ. Put the Arabic verse in the centre, then the English translation beneath it. Highlight the phrase **فَلَنُوَلِّيَنَّكَ قِبْلَةً تَرْضَاهَا** in a subtle gold tone. Keep the slide reverent and minimalistic.

### Arabic

قَدْ نَرَىٰ تَقَلُّبَ وَجْهِكَ فِي السَّمَاءِ ۖ فَلَنُوَلِّيَنَّكَ قِبْلَةً تَرْضَاهَا ۚ فَوَلِّ وَجْهَكَ شَطْرَ الْمَسْجِدِ الْحَرَامِ ۚ

### English translation

**Al-Baqarah 2:144**

"Indeed, We see you turning your face towards heaven. Now We will make you turn towards a direction of prayer that will please you. So turn your face towards the Sacred Mosque."

### Teacher notes

Explain that the Prophet ﷺ had been looking towards the heavens, awaiting Allah's command concerning the qiblah.

Allah says:

**"We see you turning your face towards heaven..."**

Then Allah commands the Muslims to face the Sacred Mosque in Makkah.

Say:

> "SubhanAllah. Allah knew what would please His Messenger ﷺ."

> "Allah saw him waiting."

> "And Allah gave him the direction of prayer that would please him."

The qiblah remains the direction Muslims face in Salah around the world today.

## Allah addresses His Prophets

Explain that the Qur'an sometimes addresses Prophets by name:

* **يَا نُوحُ** — O Nuh
* **يَا إِبْرَاهِيمُ** — O Ibrahim
* **يَا مُوسَىٰ** — O Musa
* **يَا عِيسَىٰ** — O Isa

When addressing Muhammad ﷺ, the Qur'an uses honouring titles such as:

* **يَا أَيُّهَا النَّبِيُّ** — O Prophet
* **يَا أَيُّهَا الرَّسُولُ** — O Messenger

**Teacher note:** Do not turn this into an absolute claim that Allah never names Muhammad ﷺ in the Qur'an. His name does appear in the Qur'an. The point is that direct forms of address such as "O Prophet" and "O Messenger" reflect his honoured position.

### Transition

> "We have looked at his character and the impact of his life. Now let's look at another category of evidence: the miracles Allah gave him."

# 5:48–5:55 — HIS MIRACLES

## SLIDE 10 — Miracles: signs from Allah

**Slide description for Gemini:**

Create a dramatic but dignified overview slide with five simple visual symbols: a moon, water, food, raised hands for du'a, and a historic battlefield silhouette. Use dark navy and muted gold. Do not depict the Prophet ﷺ. Label the slide **"Miracles are signs from Allah"** and list the five examples below.

### Teacher introduction

A **mu'jizah** is an extraordinary sign Allah grants a Prophet in support of his claim to prophethood.

Make one thing clear:

> **"The Prophet ﷺ did not possess independent divine power. Allah was the One who performed the miracles through His will."**

## 1. The splitting of the moon

Allah says:

**Arabic**

اقْتَرَبَتِ السَّاعَةُ وَانشَقَّ الْقَمَرُ

**English**

"The Hour has drawn near and the moon has split."

**Surah al-Qamar 54:1**

Reports in Sahih al-Bukhari and Sahih Muslim describe the companions witnessing the moon split during the Prophet's ﷺ lifetime.

Explain the significance:

The moon is a celestial body visible to people across the world. The reported event was not an ordinary occurrence like a person predicting rain or performing a clever trick.

Ask:

> "Imagine being in Makkah, looking at the moon, and witnessing something extraordinary that the Prophet ﷺ identifies as a sign."

> "How would you react?"

Emphasise that the Islamic case relies on the Qur'anic verse and the transmitted reports, not merely on a modern photograph or a claim that NASA has confirmed the event.

## 2. Water flowing from his fingers

This is one of the most striking reports.

Authentic narrations describe water flowing from between the Prophet's ﷺ fingers, allowing his companions to drink and perform wudu.

One report describes around **300 people** performing wudu. Another report connected with al-Hudaybiyyah describes around **1,500 people** drinking and performing wudu from the water.

Explain the scale:

* Hundreds of people needed water.
* The available water was limited.
* The reports describe water flowing from between his fingers.
* The companions were able to use that water.

Ask:

> "Where did the extra water come from?"

Answer: **Allah provided it as a miracle for His Messenger ﷺ.**

**References:** Sahih al-Bukhari, reports concerning the signs of prophethood and al-Hudaybiyyah; Sahih Muslim also contains reports of water flowing from his fingers.

## 3. Food increasing through barakah

Reports from the seerah and authentic hadith collections describe small quantities of food being sufficient for unexpectedly large groups through Allah's blessing.

Discuss the principle:

> "A small amount of food normally feeds a small number of people. But in these reports, the food was sufficient for far more than people would ordinarily expect."

Mention the expedition of Tabuk as part of the broader seerah discussion of hardship, provisions and Allah's help, but avoid assigning a particular food-increase story to Tabuk unless you have the exact narration ready. Several food-blessing miracles occurred at different times.

## 4. Du'as being answered

The Prophet ﷺ made supplications that Allah answered in remarkable ways.

Explain that these events were not the Prophet acting independently of Allah. They were signs of Allah's response to the supplication of His Messenger.

Also clarify:

> "This does not mean that every du'a must be answered in exactly the way or at the exact moment a person requests. Allah answers with His wisdom."

## 5. Victory despite overwhelming odds

The early Muslims faced enemies who often had greater numbers and material resources.

The Battle of Badr is an important example of a victory in which the Qur'an describes Allah's help.

However, do not teach that every victory was automatically a miracle or that the Muslims never planned or prepared.

The Prophet ﷺ consulted his companions, made plans and took practical precautions.

### Summary line

> **"He showed remarkable character. He brought a message that transformed society. And Allah supported him with extraordinary signs."**

# 5:55–6:04 — HIS PROPHECIES

## SLIDE 11 — Predictions that came true

**Slide description for Gemini:**

Create a timeline-style slide with five sections: **The Romans**, **Islam's spread**, **Tall buildings**, **Leadership and trials**, and **Events after his death**. Use a subtle visual for each: ancient Roman architecture, a map, a skyline, a leadership silhouette and a calendar. Keep claims concise. Avoid modern political figures or implying a specific person is the fulfilment of a prophecy.

### Teacher introduction

Say:

> "A miracle is an extraordinary sign. A prophecy is a statement about something that will happen in the future."

> "Let's look at predictions attributed to the Prophet ﷺ and to the Qur'an, and consider why Muslims see them as evidence."

## 1. The Romans would recover

### Arabic

غُلِبَتِ الرُّومُ ۝٢

فِي أَدْنَى الْأَرْضِ وَهُمْ مِنْ بَعْدِ غَلَبِهِمْ سَيَغْلِبُونَ ۝٣

فِي بِضْعِ سِنِينَ ۗ لِلَّهِ الْأَمْرُ مِنْ قَبْلُ وَمِنْ بَعْدُ ۚ وَيَوْمَئِذٍ يَفْرَحُ الْمُؤْمِنُونَ ۝٤

### English translation

**Surah al-Rum 30:2–4**

"The Romans have been defeated in a nearby land. Yet following their defeat, they will triumph within three to nine years. The whole matter rests with Allah before and after."

Explain that the Qur'an announced the Romans' defeat and predicted that they would triumph again within a specified period. The Byzantine Empire subsequently recovered against the Sasanian Persians.

Ask:

> "Why is a prediction about a future military reversal more specific than a vague statement that something good will happen?"

Expected answer: it names the opposing powers and gives a timeframe.

## 2. Islam would spread

The Prophet ﷺ foretold the spread and strength of Islam.

Explain the starting point:

* A small community in Makkah.
* A minority facing persecution.
* Limited political and military power at the beginning.

Yet Islam spread throughout Arabia and beyond.

**Teacher note:** This is part of the cumulative argument. Historical expansion alone does not prove that a religion is true, but Muslims consider it alongside the other evidence for the Prophet ﷺ.

## 3. People would compete in constructing tall buildings

A famous hadith about the signs of the Hour describes barefoot, poor shepherds competing in the construction of tall buildings.

**Reference:** Sahih Muslim, the hadith of Jibril concerning Islam, iman, ihsan and the signs of the Hour.

Ask:

> "Why is this striking?"

The report describes people from a humble, pastoral background competing in ambitious construction.

Explain that Muslims often see modern skyscraper competition in parts of the Arabian Peninsula as a striking resemblance to this description. Avoid claiming that one particular building or country definitively fulfils the prophecy.

## 4. Authority would be entrusted to the unqualified

In a famous hadith, the Prophet ﷺ says that when authority is entrusted to those unfit for it, one should expect the Hour.

**Reference:** Sahih al-Bukhari, the hadith concerning the loss of trust and the placing of affairs in the hands of those unqualified.

Ask:

> "What happens to a society when people are given responsibility they are not capable of carrying?"

Expected answers:

* Poor decisions.
* Corruption.
* Injustice.
* People losing trust.

Explain that this is a warning about the importance of responsibility and competence. Do not use it as a simplistic label for a modern individual or political leader.

## 5. Events after the Prophet's ﷺ death

Briefly mention three types of prediction.

### The death of Fatimah رضي الله عنها

During his final illness, the Prophet ﷺ told his daughter Fatimah رضي الله عنها privately that she would be the first of his family to follow him after his death.

She died several months after him.

**Reference:** Sahih al-Bukhari, hadith 3715–3716.

### Martyrdom and trials

The Prophet ﷺ foretold the deaths of particular companions and warned of trials that would affect the Muslim community after his death. Specific reports should be studied individually, with attention to their wording and authenticity.

### Political conflict and division

The Prophet ﷺ warned that the Muslim community would face trials and political conflict after his death. The early Muslim community experienced major disagreements and conflicts during the period of the caliphs.

### Teacher summary

> "We have looked at predictions involving empires, the spread of Islam, social changes and events within the Muslim community."

> "The important thing is to examine each prediction carefully: what was actually said, how reliable the report is, and how the event compares with the prediction."
